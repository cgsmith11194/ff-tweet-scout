A detailed geological classification: 1. Wheeler Geologic Area (Colorado, USA): These towers and columns originated from volcanic tuff associated with the La Garita Caldera (formed approximately 25 million years ago). Layers of solidified volcanic ash, varying in hardness, were eroded unevenly by wind and water. 2. Hierve el Agua (Oaxaca, Mexico): These "petrified waterfalls" are not melted walls, but rather massive travertine deposits. Over millennia, spring water rich in calcium carbonate flowed down the cliffs, depositing white minerals—a process similar to the formation of stalactites and stalagmites in caves. 3. Goblin Valley State Park ("Three Sisters," Utah, USA): These "hoodoos" (earth pyramids) are composed of Entrada Sandstone. Because the upper rock layers are significantly harder and more resistant to erosion than the softer sandstone beneath them, these mushroom-like or figure-like "heads" remain standing. 4. Red Rock Canyon State Park (Cantil, California, USA): Here, sedimentary rocks—consisting of sandstone and clay—meet darker layers of basalt and lava. Tectonic uplift, combined with strong desert winds and flash floods, has eroded the softer intermediate layers, causing harder rock blocks to slide downward. 5. Drumheller Hoodoos (Alberta, Canada): Located in the Red Deer River Valley, these sandstone pillars date back 70 to 75 million years (to the Cretaceous period). In this case, an exceptionally hard capstone made of ironstone protects the softer sandstone column beneath it from being washed away by rain and meltwater. P.S. (I upscaled the photos a bit.)
