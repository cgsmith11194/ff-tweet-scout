In 2009 he ran for 2,006 yards, making him just the sixth man in NFL history to break 2,000 in a single season. Track speed, cutback vision, gone before the defense knew what happened. This week he wrote a letter to his four children in The Players' Tribune. He wrote it because ALS is taking his ability to speak. It started last year with weakness in his right hand. On a family trip he noticed his grip going. By this year he needs a computerized device to talk. "How can a man just be perfectly healthy one day, running around with his kids in the yard, and then the next, your hand starts getting weak, and it just travels throughout your whole body until you can't talk anymore? Man, I've asked myself that question like a million times, and I still don't have an answer." Johnson has been to nearly every major ALS clinic in the country. He's read the study that found former NFL players are nearly four times more likely to develop and die from ALS than other men. He knows what it might mean. He wrote about it honestly, but he refused to be bitter about the game. "I wouldn't trade that journey for anything. Football gave your dad a platform. And now, I'm in a position to use it in a way I never expected." Most of the letter isn't about football at all. It's about them. "All four of y'all, promise me you'll always stick together. Walking his daughter down the aisle. Seeing his boys become men. Meeting his grandkids. Growing old with Brittany. He called his kids the biggest reason he keeps fighting.
