Then mineral-rich water finds its way in, slowly, and leaves its cargo behind on the walls, a microscopic layer at a time. Do that for a span of time measured in thousands of years, sometimes far more, and you get a chamber of crystals sealed inside a plain, dull shell. Think about how strange that is. The outside of the stone is shaped by weather, abrasion, rain, rivers, the slow violence of ordinary erosion. The inside is shaped by stillness. One side of the same object has been battered by the world for ages. The other side has been growing in complete darkness, undisturbed, for just as long, and has never once been seen by anyone. Two histories, one object, separated by a few centimeters of rind. And the rind is not an accident. The hard outer layer is often precisely why the cavity survived at all. Soft surroundings wear away. The tough shell is what lets the quiet interior exist long enough to be found. The ugly part is the protection. The dull exterior and the hidden interior are not opposites. They are collaborators. THE LARGEST SECRETS EVER OPENED If this sounds like a small-scale curiosity, look at the extreme end of the scale.
