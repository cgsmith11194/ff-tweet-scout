I'll explain why this is poorly done and doesn't support the title (help from supporting cast). 1. If you agree sacks are a QB stat, then so are pressures. Having a low pressure rate because you routinely get the ball out quickly doesn't imply your protection is great. 2. Separation is a junk stat. It might be useful information if dug into deeper, but presented as an average yardage figure, it gives us nothing useful to say about QBs or even their supporting cast. To say nothing of a QB who can create separation by throwing on the move and using their eyes and pump fakes to create even more separation for the receiver they decide to throw to. 3. Drop % - Too basic; drops aren't created equally. An offense with twice as many drops can still be less harmful than an offense where most of the drops are on deep balls and 3rd/4th downs that would have extended drives. A lot of drops on screens, early downs, and would be failed completions on 3rd and long simply don't hurt the QB as much. 4. YAC - Too basic; YAC isn't created equally. Need some kind of expected YAC component so you're not just dinging a QB for the style of offense he's in. If you throw more screens and passes behind the LOS and check downs, you're going to end up with more YAC because of where the ball is being caught. That doesn't mean a 3-yard gain with 11 YAC on a 3rd-and-15 is a good play. For that matter an 8-yard pass with no YAC on 3rd-and-15 usually isn't a good play either. I'm not saying he did basic bitch work because he's a Patriots fan. I'd say these exact same things if he was a fan of any team. These things as they were used do not actually tell us anything about the QB's supporting cast, nor do they do enough to separate the QB's performance from making these numbers what they are.
