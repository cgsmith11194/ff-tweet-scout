I do agree the concept of clear lead RBs being reduced is largely overblown. Teams still want their best RBs on the field as often as possible. But if you adjust these opportunity shares on a games played basis (min. 8 gms, controlling for injury), the numbers look a lot flatter across the board (65% of touches and at higher thresholds). I would definitely not say that "bellcow" usage is rising in the NFL though. While % of RBs earning 60-65% of opportunities has risen a bit and did hit a career-high these past 2 years even when controlling for injury (17-18, 15 was prior high), the 75-80%+ of opportunities group is notably short of the peak number in this past decade (4 last year, 8 is peak). IMO, the recent decline of Zero RB and why the top of the position has been so successful boils down to two things: 1. Positive injury luck at the top of RB position, along with more WR injuries than we've seen in the past decade (Zero RB teams are built on the opposite happening) 2. Major dips in RB prices during this time (2023-2025 RBs easily cheapest of past decade). Sounds counter-intuitive, but cheaper RB prices defeats the purpose of zero RB as all drafters are getting better prices on them AND it causes WR to get priced up. This eliminates the edge of strong WR profiles going later than they should have in years where zero RB worked

The Arsenal Fit: Vinicius Junior This was an enjoyable analysis to make because of the hype surrounding the link. I have calmed down since last night, but I still believe this is a signing we should pursue after last season’s achievements. I hope you enjoy reading this as much as I enjoyed writing it! In simple terms; Vinicius Junior optimises the whole Arsenal team and creates a real problem for opponents. His generational talent and elite individual ability would completely change the dynamics of our frontline. Opponents would have to double up on both him and Saka, allowing the rest of the pitch and the players operating centrally to have a lot more space. It would allow us to pin teams in even more because they would be forced to defend so negatively against two World Class wingers. For years, opposing defensive lines have favoured defending Saka's side because Arsenal have not had a player on the left who reaches his level or demands the same attention. This has become even tougher as Odegaard now drops deeper and Saka has become more of a ball-to-feet player. That can continue, but he would have so much more space because of Vini. There would finally be another elite technical beast on the opposite flank. How Vini improves the rest of the team If opponents double up on Vini, Saka can receive with far more freedom and vice versa. Further, if the opp protects both sides, more space opens up centrally for Bruno G, Odegaard and Gyok. There isn’t really a clean solution for opponents. The left half space (LHS) combinations with Calafiori and Bruno G could be excellent. Vini’s elite separation and body manipulation mean he can beat almost every defender over a yard. Calafiori occupying the LHS forces the RB, RCB and opposition midfielder into difficult decisions. A brief explanation: - If the RB stays attached to Vini, Calafiori underlaps - If the RB follows Calafiori, Vini is isolated - If the RCB moves across, Gyokeres receives more room centrally. That could be especially valuable because VG’s separation inside crowded areas is not his best quality. He normally has both CBs occupied, but Vini would stop them from focusing completely on him. This would give Viktor more room to shoot and attack cutbacks and low crosses. Vini is exceptional at this as he can beat the RB outside and play left-footed deliveries across the box, while Gyokeres is a brilliant one-touch finisher. The transitional threat alongside Gyokeres and Saka would also give Arsenal a completely different approach after turnovers. Why Arteta’s system suits him Arsenal's box midfield creates a huge problem when you add Vini. Rice and Timber form the bottom of the box, with Bruno G and Odegaard higher in the half space channels. Against an opposing two or three man midfield, Arsenal create a clear central overload that allows one of Bruno or Odegaard to receive vertical progressive passes. Refer to Image 1 It gives Vini freedom to either remain wide, attack the channel, move into the LHS or wait before making an explosive run. With Odegaard's circulation and Bruno's progressive passing, Arsenal can attract pressure to one side before switching quickly. Bruno G’s importance to the left hand combo, he ranks in the: - 89th percentile for progressive passes - 93rd percentile for forward passes - 91st percentile for passes into the final third Imagine this with Vini as the receiver. We saw this on occasion for Brazil this World Cup. Bruno can find him early before the block is organised, play vertically into the LHS or release him in behind with Vini's elite channel running. Another important note: Rice’s role would turn more conservative and would see him and Timber provide the rest defence behind Ode and Bruno G. With Saliba and Gabi, they create a structured and secure 2-2 base. Timber and Rice may have to sacrifice some attacking freedom and intent but the trade-off is worth it IMO. Refer to Image 1 Vini’s individual dribbling and carrying profile Vini is an elite take-on and separation dribbler who uses his low centre of gravity, agility and balance to attack defenders on their unbalanced side. His acceleration and deceleration force defenders to open their hips and break their momentum. He can operate from the touchline or as an inside forward in the LHS. When he starts wide, he pins the RB and uses the touchline as his launch pad. He can take a player on while receiving statically or with momentum. His acceleration is insane. He can go from 0 to 100 in two or three steps, and barely any RB has the body dynamism to cope repeatedly. Maybe Timber, but luckily we already have him. If the opp RB covers inside, Vini can accelerate outside and play a dangerous left footed cutback. If the RB covers wide and protects the byline, Vini can shift onto his right foot and attack the LHS. When he moves inside (either off-the-ball or whilst dribbling) he becomes a direct goal and creative threat, using his balance and body manipulation to create separation before shooting or combining with killer through balls Further, The numbers support the eye test. He ranked in the 100th percentile among La Liga left wingers for creating his own shots and progressive carries, as well as the 88th percentile for successful dribbles and 77th percentile for carries into the final third (per-90 streamlit app). His 44.6% domestic dribble success rate needs context. Vini is a high rep dribbler who does not protect his percentage through safe attempts. He will lose possession, but he will continue attacking the RB. It is tunnel vision in all the right ways. His dispossessions increased from 1.40 per 90 in La Liga to 1.77 in the UCL, showing the retention cost attached to his style. As mentioned in my prior post, his physical data also surprised me. In La Liga, he won 6.28 duels per 90 with a 48.9% success rate, ranking in the 71st and 88th percentiles. He clearly does not shy away from contact, while his balance allows him to absorb challenges. He also won 2.58 fouls per 90, ranking in the 93rd percentile. Crucial for our abilities from set-pieces and winning penalties. His UCL numbers dropped against stronger opposition: 2.08 dribbles and 4.69 duels won per 90. That may suggest a minor adjustment to Premier League physicality, although he still ranked in the 93rd percentile for dribbles and 83rd for fouls won (2.08 per 90). Shooting and Big Chance Data Across La Liga and the UCL, Vini scored 21 goals from 21.74 xG, including 5 penalties. His overall and non-penalty output were almost exactly in line with expectation, with 16 non-penalty goals from approximately 16.21 npxG. He attempted 157 shots, averaging 3.54 per 90. 66 hit the target, giving him a 42% SOT rate, while his overall goal conversion rate was 13.4%. Nearly 74% of his shots came from inside the box where scored 19/116 attempts. Despite being capable of spectacular goals, he is mainly a repetitive box focused shooter. There was a clear contrast between the domestic and continental numbers. In La Liga, he scored 16 goals from 13.65 xG, had a 46% SOT rate and a 16% goal conversion rate. In the UCL, he scored 5 from 8.09 xG, a massive -3.09xG goal underperformance. His SOT rate dropped to 36% and his goal conversion rate to 9%, despite his shot volume increasing from 3.22 per 90 (La Liga) to 4.31 in the UCL. However, his xGOT data adds context: when he hit the target, his placement was generally strong and several high-quality efforts were saved. The issue is therefore consistency rather than a lack of elite finishing ability. Creativity and Transitions Vini is not only an elite dribbler and goalscorer. In La Liga, he recorded 5 assists from 5.6 xA (97th percentile) and averaged 2.23 key passes per 90 (96th percentile).
