IMO, assuming small WRs can’t score is mistaking correlation with causation, and the reasoning is more so that the crop of really good NFL WRs tends to not be littered with a lot of smaller guys. Among WRs since 2010 with 110+ targets in a season, “small” WRs (shorter than 5’11”, lighter than 195 lbs) have averaged 7.11 TDs per season, and 3.04 of those on average came from passes inside the 10 yard line. The “others” (5’11 or taller + 196+ lbs) have averaged 7.06 TDs, and 3.01 of them coming from inside the 10 targets. So when controlling for high volume WRs, the TD discrepancy between small and non-small WR is non-existent. The small WRs in this 110+ target group also had an 8+ TD season 41.1% of the time, while the others are behind at 38.9%. Once controlling for targets, size adds an additional 0.006 of r-squared to TD scoring. In simpler terms, size explains just an additional 0.6% more of the variance of TD scoring beyond just total targets plus inside the 10 targets alone (established above that i10 targets are largely not based on size, but rather talent).
